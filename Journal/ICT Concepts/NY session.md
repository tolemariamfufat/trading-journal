### 1. London Session as the Trend Setter

The London session often sets the initial direction or trend for the day. If the market is moving lower during London, it is typically seeking a specific "draw on liquidity" or a Higher Time Frame objective (such as a Daily or Weekly level of support or a liquidity pool).
### 2. NY Session as the Continuation

The New York session is fundamentally viewed as the **continuation** of the logic established in London.
- **The Scenario:** If London was bearish (going down) but the price has **not yet reached** the Daily (D1) or Weekly (W1) HTF objective.
- **The Expectation:** You expect a short-term retracement (a "pullback") at the beginning of the NY session, followed by a **continuation** of the London trend.
- **The Goal:** The algorithm seeks to finish the job started in London by reaching that HTF objective during the NY morning or lunch hour [[02:28:04](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tub1JHNOIB0&t=8884)].
### 3. The NY Reversal Profile
The "reversal profile" is triggered only once the objective is hit.
- **When to expect it:** If the NY session successfully drives the price into the D1 or W1 HTF objective (the target), the expansion move is considered complete.
- **The Shift:** At this point, the market has no more immediate reason to continue in that direction. The NY session then shifts from a "continuation profile" to a **"reversal profile,"** where price may reverse or consolidate for the remainder of the session [[02:26:10](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tub1JHNOIB0&t=8770)].
### Summary of the Logic:
- **Objective NOT met:** NY = Retrace $\rightarrow$ Continuation (Trend Following).
- **Objective MET:** NY = Potential Reversal or End of Trend (Trend Termination).
This framework allows a trader to anticipate whether they should be looking for a continuation trade or preparing for a market turn based on where the price sits relative to the Higher Time Frame targets.
